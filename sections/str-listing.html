Core Workflow

STR Listing

Navigation FT AML → STR Listing

The register of every Suspicious Transaction Report the institution has filed. Where Case Manager shows the investigation, STR Listing shows the regulatory output of that investigation — one row per filing.

What It Tracks

Each row represents one case that has produced an STR, linking the case back to the STR reference, who raised it, and the transaction subjects and amount involved — giving the MLRO a single register of everything that has been reported, without needing to reopen each case individually.

How to use

  • Search by Case Title, Case ID, or STR ID
  • Use Show Filters to narrow the list further
  • Click Download CSV on any row to export that filing's details
  • Click a row to jump back into the originating case

How an STR gets filed, end to end: STR Listing is a read-only register — there is no separate "create STR" form to fill in here. A filing is always the output of a case that has already been investigated in Case Manager, produced through a guided, GoTRACS-compliant filing wizard. The full process runs as follows:

  • Investigate the case — an analyst reviews the alert(s) behind the case, as described in Case Queues → View Case, and documents findings that support their conclusion
  • Reach a True Match disposition — when the evidence supports suspicious activity, the analyst sets the case disposition to True Match and selects Escalate for SAR/STR, rather than closing it as a False Match, per Case Lifecycle, which launches the STR filing wizard

The wizard itself runs across six steps:

  • Step 1 — Select Alerts — choose which of the case's alerts the filing should cover; the transactions and subject parties from the selected alerts carry through automatically to later steps
  • Step 2 — Report Type — choose the STR Type (e.g. STRR Regular, STRHP High Priority, STRBR Bulk Report, STRA Per Account, STRHU Highly Unusual), confirm the auto-filled Institution code and Supervising agency, and set the Submission type and STR trigger (e.g. KYC Docs requested by AMLC Referrals) and Date of determination
  • Step 3 — Reason for Suspicion — select one or more Suspicious Circumstance codes (SC1–SC6) that best describe why the activity is suspicious, plus any Additional Reason Codes needed beyond the primary selection
  • Step 4 — Transaction — review the suspicious transactions triggered by the selected alerts, all included by default; deselect any that should be excluded from the filing, and optionally narrow by Type or Period From/To. The total amount across selected transactions is shown live
  • Step 5 — Subject Parties — review the parties auto-derived from the selected transactions (sender and receiver); click a party to expand and confirm or edit their identity details — Party role, Entity type, name fields, CRN/account number, ID type and number, and date of birth
  • Step 6 — Narratives — write the STR Narrative explaining the basis for suspicion in the analyst's own words; a live character counter confirms the entry meets the regulator's recommended length, and the alert(s) covered by the filing are listed for reference before submission
  • MLRO review and approval — once submitted, the draft routes to the Compliance Officer / MLRO, the only role with sign-off authority on a filing (see Roles & Access); they review the evidence and either approve it or send it back to the analyst for further work
  • Filing is recorded — once approved, the filing appears as a new row in STR Listing with its own STR ID, and the originating case's Status updates to reflect that a report has been created against it
  • Submit to the regulator — the approved filing is submitted to the relevant Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) through the institution's regulatory reporting channel; Download CSV exports the filing's underlying transaction and subject detail to support that submission and for internal record-keeping
  • Retained for audit — the filing, its supporting case file, and every action taken along the way remain permanently linked and retrievable, satisfying the record-keeping obligations that apply to filed reports
NoteBecause every filing traces back to a specific case, STR Listing should be treated as a reporting and audit register rather than a workspace — any correction or follow-up investigation happens by reopening the case itself in Case Manager, not by editing the STR row directly.

STR Listing Columns

Column What it shows
Case Title / ID The customer or subject name and the case that produced this STR
STR ID A sequential internal reference number for the filing
Created At Date and time the STR was generated
Created By The analyst or MLRO who raised the filing
Subjects Every individual or entity named in the STR — a filing can name more than one subject if a case involves linked parties
Amount The cumulative transaction value associated with the filing
Download CSV Exports the filing's underlying transaction and subject detail for submission or record-keeping
Example

A single STR listing three subjects and an amount of ₹747,569.31 tells the MLRO, at a glance, that this was a multi-party case — worth opening before smaller single-subject filings when prioritizing a regulator review.
